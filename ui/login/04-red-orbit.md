# 04 — Red Orbit

[Open the composition study](visuals/04-red-orbit.svg).

**The image in your head**

A large circular composition sits on black, with ten distinct small marks distributed around its edge and a crimson cloud at the center. The ring is cropped by the left edge of the screen, giving it scale. A sharply composed login column sits to the right. The page feels like individual strengths gathering around a shared purpose.

The Akatsuki reference comes from the ring symbolism and collective structure. The marks are abstract identity symbols, not invented Japanese writing or character statistics.

**Desktop composition**

Divide the page asymmetrically: approximately 60% circular graphic and campaign copy, 40% form. The orbit has a diameter around 680px at a 1440px viewport. Its center sits slightly below the page midpoint, leaving a clean upper area for the wordmark and headline.

Use **“Different strengths. One collective.”** as the campaign headline. Place it above the circle rather than along a curved path. A short line below explains opportunities and team formation. All essential copy reads left-to-right in a conventional order.

The circle contains a broad empty center with one large cloud. Ten restrained nodes around the perimeter create rhythm; alternate small geometric shapes and simple radial strokes. Two arc segments in crimson provide emphasis. The form sits on a solid, slightly lighter surface with a clean outer boundary.

**Material, color, and type**

Use black `#101012`, graphite `#242429`, chalk `#F2EEE7`, red `#D1374E`, and muted silver `#A6A5AD`. Geometry is flat and precise, with no metallic gradients or sci-fi glow.

Explore Space Grotesk or a similar geometric display sans with a neutral UI family. Use generous letter spacing only for short eyebrows. Round shape language belongs in the artwork; keep fields as familiar rounded rectangles.

**The signature detail**

The ring is incomplete, with a deliberate opening facing the form. This makes the graphic feel like an invitation and directs the eye toward the sign-in action. The opening should be visually apparent without an explanatory animation.

**Mobile transformation**

Reduce the ring to an off-center crop behind the header, about 140–180px tall. Keep three or four nodes and the cloud visible. The form then occupies the full available width on a solid surface.

Do not shrink the complete circle into a tiny diagram above the form. Preserve the gesture of the open ring, and allow more of the artwork on taller devices. The remaining ring is decorative and absent from the tab order.

**Interaction and states**

For an optional entrance, let two arc segments resolve into position over about 350ms. Do not rotate the ring indefinitely or make users interact with it to reveal the form. Form focus does not light nodes as if students were completing a game.

Loading stays on the submit button. The ring is never an authentication progress meter. Errors remain readable below the affected fields, and the solid panel grows independently from the artwork.

**Art brief and assets**

Create the complete visual as vector geometry: one cloud-inspired mark, two arcs, ten restrained node symbols, and a light radial structure. Prepare wide and mobile layouts separately. Avoid faux technical readouts, ranks, invented member data, and decorative kanji.

**What carries into the app**

The open-ring shape can frame avatars, category illustrations, or small brand moments. Distinct nodes could inform category icons only after a consistent mapping is defined. Never imply student rankings, verified skill levels, or achievements from decorative symbols.

**Risks and refinement questions**

Radial graphics can drift into cryptocurrency, gaming, or an occult aesthetic. Keep the geometry clear, use ordinary product language, and test recognition without mentioning the concept name. The purpose of the ring is community, not exclusivity.

**Prototype priority:** medium. This is a strong vector-only alternative when symbolic identity matters more than texture or illustration.
