# 03 — Paper Rebellion

[Open the composition study](visuals/03-paper-rebellion.svg).

**The image in your head**

A bold campus poster covers a warm paper background. Huge black lettering, a flat crimson cloud, a few purposeful overprints, and a clean black login panel make the page feel like a student collective launching its first issue. The energy is outspoken and creative, with enough warmth to welcome someone browsing between lectures.

This explores Akatsuki through graphic identity and printed material. It provides a bright alternative within the same crimson-and-black world.

**Desktop composition**

Use an off-white canvas with generous outside margins. Place a thin top rule under the wordmark and a small descriptor. Below, split the page into a large editorial area on the left and a black rectangular form panel on the right, roughly 58/42.

The left headline is the main artwork: **“YOUR PEOPLE. YOUR NEXT MOVE.”** Use four tightly composed lines, with “NEXT” partly backed by a crimson rectangular overprint. A cloud sits behind the lower part of the letterforms, but never reduces legibility.

Below the headline, add a concise three-part statement with strong spacing: “Find a role. Form a team. Make something.” A tiny footer line can read “A campus collective for people with ideas.” Keep text horizontal and readable; small production-style labels stay decorative and sparse.

The form panel has generous internal padding and almost square corners. It contains the shared heading, labels, fields, button, and identity explanation. The panel's height follows content, including errors.

**Material, color, and type**

Use paper `#EEE8DC`, ink `#171715`, crimson `#BE263D`, muted paper `#D5CCBC`, and white `#FAF7F1`. Add extremely faint grain only to the background, never to inputs or text.

Explore a condensed display face such as Barlow Condensed for the poster and a readable sans such as Manrope for the interface. The display should feel deliberate and contemporary, with a strong difference between headline and body sizes. Use a single consistent body typeface across the product.

**The signature detail**

Give the big cloud a slight print-registration offset: an ivory or black edge peeks out a few pixels from one side. Carry the same offset into one oversized campaign word. Keep all functional text crisp and precisely aligned. This becomes a recognizable visual language rather than a distressed effect applied everywhere.

**Mobile transformation**

Keep the paper header and wordmark, then show a compact two-line campaign title with a cropped cloud. The black form panel becomes full width inside 16–20px page margins. The top art area should occupy about a quarter of the initial screen at most.

At 320px, remove decorative production labels and reduce display size. Let the form panel continue vertically with the page; do not give it an internal scrollbar. Large text must never be clipped to preserve a poster shape.

**Interaction and states**

Use a small button translation or contrast change for hover, respecting reduced motion. A tiny printed cloud can settle into place during the initial reveal, but the content is immediately visible. Form errors use a clear icon and text; red is already a brand color and cannot communicate error by itself.

Success is understated: the button confirms completion and navigation proceeds. Avoid stamping “APPROVED” across the page, which would confuse authentication with acceptance into a role.

**Art brief and assets**

Build this concept primarily from real typography, vector shapes, a custom cloud mark, and optional lightweight paper texture. It needs no hero image. Supply layout variants with and without the grain to verify that texture contributes meaningfully.

**What carries into the app**

Editorial headings, ruled separators, black action panels, paper-toned surfaces, and crimson chips can become the product's design system. This direction offers a natural light theme, with a matching charcoal treatment for Messages if tested carefully.

**Risks and refinement questions**

Excessively condensed type or too many stamps can become a music-festival poster. Keep the functional area sober and the page hierarchy clear. Ask whether the bright direction still reads as Akatsuki and whether students prefer it for daytime campus use.

**Prototype priority:** high. This is the most distinctive counterpoint to Crimson Fold, and the most economical direction to prototype faithfully.
