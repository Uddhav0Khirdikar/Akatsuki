# 06 — Signal Board

[Open the composition study](visuals/06-signal-board.svg).

**The image in your head**

A dark campus noticeboard holds a small collection of designed posters, opportunity snippets, crimson cloud stickers, and student-made marks. A clean ivory sign-in sheet occupies the right side. The whole scene suggests that interesting people are already making things here, while being honest that the examples are illustrative.

The Akatsuki collective becomes a campus creative culture: individual contributions share one recognizable identity.

**Desktop composition**

Use a dark background and a balanced 56/44 split. The left side contains three or four large overlapping objects arranged with deliberate hierarchy. The right side contains an upright, unrotated ivory form panel around 420px wide.

Start the left area with **“Someone on campus needs your kind of talent.”** Below it, place an editorial opportunity poster, a small project note, and an abstract cloud print. Label the cluster “Example opportunities.” Suggested content includes “Build a campus app,” “Join a robotics project,” and “Design for a student team.”

Keep the examples broad and clearly fictional; use no invented student photos, names, unread counters, or live application counts. Two cards may be rotated by a few degrees, while the main headline and form remain perfectly horizontal. Show enough background around the collage that its silhouettes remain readable.

**Material, color, and type**

Use charcoal `#17171B`, paper `#F0EBE2`, red `#CA3047`, dusty lavender `#B8ADC4` as a tiny secondary accent, and warm gray `#9E9996`. Limit the palette of each individual object so the collage remains coherent.

Use a strong display sans for posters, a clear body sans for all interface text, and an optional handwritten accent for one short decorative note. The handwritten style cannot carry functional instructions or form labels.

The form panel uses dark text, light inputs with clear borders, and a crimson submit button. Give it a small cloud stamp in its header, with no literal punched holes, folded corner covering content, or fake physical input controls.

**The signature detail**

A crimson line or narrow tape strip visually links the opportunity posters to the sign-in sheet. Its final segment aligns with the form heading. This makes the composition feel like joining the activity on the board rather than entering a separate administrative portal.

**Mobile transformation**

Compress the collage into a shallow header containing one large cloud print and one example-opportunity edge. Put the readable product explanation above the form. Most of the sample content can disappear at this width; the essential promise remains in real text.

Use a full-width ivory form surface with generous padding. Avoid a horizontal carousel of example opportunities before sign-in. On mobile, the collage is an identity cue rather than content students must navigate.

**Interaction and states**

Keep example posters noninteractive unless they eventually link to genuine accessible listings. A small one-time settling motion can position a sticker, but cards do not drift or respond to pointer tilt. Decorative elements should not imply that users can drag or collect them.

The form panel grows down for validation and service notices. Error messages use iconography and text as well as color. The background collage stays still during submission so attention remains on the result.

**Art brief and assets**

Create a small, curated set of vector posters, one cloud sticker, a paper-edge treatment, and optional minimal grain. Keep opportunity text as accessible HTML in a future prototype where it conveys product information. Separate decorative layers from content. More objects are not automatically better: start with three and add only when a clear compositional need appears.

**What carries into the app**

The poster language can become category headers and shareable opportunity graphics. Paper surfaces work for forms and detail views; the noticeboard metaphor could inform Explore while retaining consistent card alignment and scanability.

**Risks and refinement questions**

The collage may become visually noisy or resemble a generic student club recruitment page. The design needs an unusually strong type hierarchy and disciplined crimson usage to stay recognizably Akatsuki. Confirm that students understand the sample content is illustrative.

**Prototype priority:** medium. This is the strongest direction for communicating campus community and tangible opportunities on the login page itself.
