# Concept comparison and refinement path

These assessments are design hypotheses. They guide prototyping and should be revised after student feedback.

| Direction | Strongest quality | Main risk | Artwork cost | Extension into the app |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Crimson Fold | Recognizable Akatsuki atmosphere with a calm form | Can become too dark or fashion-oriented | Medium to high for finished fabric | Strong: seams, tonal surfaces, cloud crops |
| After Hours | Emotional, cinematic first impression | Artwork may dominate product comprehension | Highest | Moderate: retain palette and typography |
| Paper Rebellion | Distinctive student energy and daytime readability | Can drift into festival-poster styling | Low to medium | Strong: type, rules, paper surfaces |
| Red Orbit | Clear symbolic composition and scalable geometry | Can suggest gaming or crypto | Low to medium | Good: restrained motifs and icon language |
| Cloud Passage | Immediate brand recognition and central focus | Difficult form growth inside decorative shape | Medium | Good: crops and quiet interior surfaces |
| Signal Board | Makes the campus purpose tangible | Collage can distract or imply live content | Medium | Strong: categories and shareable posters |

**Recommended first comparison**

Prototype **Crimson Fold**, **Paper Rebellion**, and **Cloud Passage** first. Together they test three substantially different ideas: tactile darkness, expressive lightness, and a bold centered graphic. They also establish useful directions without requiring the large illustration investment of After Hours.

Keep After Hours as the cinematic challenger. If the first three feel too graphic or restrained after review, give it a dedicated art pass. Red Orbit and Signal Board are valuable alternatives if student feedback favors symbolic belonging or explicit campus context.

**What to refine within each shortlisted direction**

| Direction | Version A | Version B | What the comparison answers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Crimson Fold | Oversized cloud on a broad, quiet fold | Strong diagonal seam with a smaller cloud | Does the brand read better through symbol scale or silhouette? |
| Paper Rebellion | Black form panel on paper canvas | Paper form area with a large red poster alongside | Does the dark form improve focus or create too much visual separation? |
| Cloud Passage | Thick open cloud contour | Layered cloud silhouette around a wider quiet center | Which keeps the form readable while preserving the bold gesture? |

Change one major compositional variable per comparison. Keep copy, field count, viewport, and test state constant. Explore color tuning after hierarchy and form placement work.

**The first high-fidelity review set**

For each of the three shortlisted directions, produce a 1440 × 900 desktop view and a 390 × 844 mobile view. Add invalid-credential and ERP-unavailable states to the mobile version. Include a 1280 × 720 desktop crop to reveal layouts that depend on excess height.

Review the still screens first. Then add a restrained motion pass to the strongest concept. Motion should reinforce a good layout rather than conceal an unresolved one.

**Questions for students**

1. What do you think this app helps you do?
2. What would you click or type first?
3. What account do you think you need?
4. What feels welcoming, and what feels confusing or overly exclusive?
5. Which version would you recognize tomorrow?
6. Which version would you be comfortable using regularly on your phone?

Ask for unaided interpretation before describing Akatsuki or explaining a concept. Include fans and non-fans, students seeking their first opportunity, and students recruiting peers.

**Decision rubric**

Weight task clarity and mobile usability most heavily. A proposed comparison uses 30% sign-in clarity, 25% mobile readability, 20% distinctive Akatsuki identity, 15% student appeal, and 10% implementation and performance practicality. These weights express priorities; a numerical score does not replace observed usability issues.

No concept advances with unreadable error states, hidden fields on small screens, misleading verification claims, or decoration that blocks interaction. Record observations alongside preferences so a popular screenshot does not override a broken sign-in flow.

**Combining concepts carefully**

A chosen direction may borrow one compatible detail from another. Crimson Fold could use Paper Rebellion's confident typography. Cloud Passage could use Signal Board's concise campus copy. Preserve the winner's layout and material language instead of adding the ring, rain, collage, fabric, and cloud opening to one page.
