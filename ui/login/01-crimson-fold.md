# 01 — Crimson Fold

[Open the composition study](visuals/01-crimson-fold.svg).

**The image in your head**

A massive fold of near-black fabric rises from the bottom-left corner. Its inner edge catches a crimson light, and a single oversized cloud appears woven into the cloth. At the right, a quiet ivory heading and a perfectly aligned login form sit in open space. The page feels like the Akatsuki cloak has become architecture.

The emotional goal is confidence and belonging: a carefully made invitation into something students want to be part of.

**Desktop composition**

Use a 12-column grid. Allocate roughly seven columns to the artwork and five to the form, separated by a generous gutter. Keep the form near 384px wide. A shared top row carries the wordmark on the left and the plain descriptor “Campus opportunity hub” on the right.

The fabric should extend outside the viewport on three edges, creating scale without framing it in a card. Place the campaign headline over a flat dark region above the fold: **“Find your people. Make your mark.”** Set it across three deliberate lines at approximately 80–96px on a wide screen. Below it, one sentence explains the product.

The cloud emblem occupies the lower left. Crop part of it so the shape feels physically large. Place the form directly on the background, with a subtle vertical rule separating its quiet zone. A small crimson eyebrow above the form says “YOUR CAMPUS. YOUR COLLECTIVE.”

**Material, color, and type**

Use ink `#0D0E11`, charcoal `#1C1D23`, oxblood `#6D172B`, crimson `#C92D45`, and ivory `#F4EFE9`. Highlight fabric through broad tonal differences rather than glossy reflections. The reference material is dense matte cloth with a faint weave visible only at large sizes.

Explore Space Grotesk for display text and Inter for the form. The headline should be bold with tight but readable spacing. Body copy stays calm and compact. Fields use a dark solid fill, visible boundaries, 8–10px corners, and off-white labels. The main button is a broad crimson rectangle.

**The signature detail**

A thin crimson seam visually connects the fabric fold to the baseline of the form's action button. It gives the two halves a shared structure. The seam is purely decorative and must not resemble a form progress indicator.

**Mobile transformation**

Compress the artwork into a top band around 180–220px tall. Keep one cropped fold and part of the cloud; remove the desktop campaign paragraph and large duplicate headline. Place a short “Find your people.” heading in that band, followed immediately by the shared form content.

The form becomes the main body on a solid background, with roughly 24px side padding. On short screens, reduce the art band further. Do not scale the desktop layout into two narrow columns.

**Interaction and states**

If motion is used, reveal the red edge once in roughly 400ms while the form is already usable. Hover can subtly brighten the button, with no cloth movement tied to input. During an error, the form grows naturally downward; the visual never shifts to accommodate it. The light seam stays decorative during loading and success.

**Art brief and assets**

Create one abstract matte-black fabric study with a deep red lining and a cloud-inspired embroidered shape. Leave the right 40% visually quiet. Supply wide and portrait crops, plus a lightweight static fallback. Compose text and fields separately from the art. A simplified vector version can establish the silhouette before investing in high-resolution material rendering.

**What carries into the app**

The seam becomes an active-navigation detail; the cloud crop becomes an empty-state motif; the matte tonal layers define surfaces. Opportunity screens inherit the calm form treatment.

**Risks and refinement questions**

The dark fabric can disappear on dim phones or look like generic luxury advertising. Test silhouette readability at low brightness and make the campus descriptor unmistakable. Avoid overly theatrical folds that resemble a game character selection screen.

**Prototype priority:** high. This is the strongest starting point for a polished, durable Akatsuki identity. The deciding test is whether students notice both the distinctive cloak reference and the practical campus purpose.
